## Tank defenses

The shared bot choreography now presses job-specific actions before Capital Punishment. Both tanks use their major defensive for the first shared sequence. The holder uses its invulnerability for each later solo sequence; the swapping tank uses Rampart plus its job's short defensive for the following Ordained Punishment. The second sequence avoids reusing the first sequence's major defensive before its ordinary cooldown would recover. These effects go through the real simulated job-action mapping and remain visible as statuses.

TEA's mitigation checks explicitly include bots. The existing 30% practice threshold for the shared hit and Ordained Punishment is coarse tuning, not a retail damage formula; a lone Capital Punishment holder needs an invulnerability. Three negative tests remove the tank bot's defensive statuses before the first hit of each sequence and require a death to Capital Punishment. The focused run passed these strict bot checks; the complete regression and packet-safety results belong in the integration report. Human action input, cooldown availability in the actual client, invulnerability timing, native damage/status presentation and tank swaps remain native acceptance checks.

The current shared job-action engine upgrades several low-level defensive button IDs to their modern status equivalents even at TEA's level 80 sync (for example Sentinel to Guardian and Heart of Stone to Heart of Corundum). Thus the bot plan proves actual simulated status checks and timing, not exact level-80 defensive potency, status presentation or block scaling. This limitation belongs to shared job-action modeling; it must not be hidden by treating bots as automatically mitigated.

## Native playback feedback correction (2026-11-11)

Ordained Motion previously used one 0.6-yalm out-and-back movement lasting about
0.6 seconds. It passed the real displacement check but was easy to miss in
playback. The client model also interpolates native movement, so rapidly reversing
short legs can be visually damped even when simulation-space movement is correct.
Bots now make repeated real 1.3-yalm alternating movements starting about 1.95
seconds before the verdict and continuing across it. Mana Final Word shuffles
perpendicular to the west/east beacon axis, preserving beacon separation. Final
Word and both Calibration α verdicts use the same scheduler, including individual
practice starts. Tests inspect native-proxy positions, accumulated travel and both travel
directions for all eight seats; a success verdict alone is not presentation
proof. No motion exemption, synthetic jump flag or changed judgment threshold
was introduced. Actual client walk visibility remains a native retest item.

The healer Optical spread already puts H1/H2 across the boss's center at x±5,
z=0; the correction pass retains those source-defined middle slots and adds a
position check. They are not assigned to the northern tank or southern DPS row.

The Nukemaru narration at 24:51–25:02 specifies entering Temporal Prison from
lowest current damage contribution, with its example showing healers, then
tanks, ranged and melee. It does not establish the suggested H2→ST→MT→H1 order
as a universal Mana priority. The practice default remains H1→H2→MT→ST→D3→D4→D1
(with D2 last outside the seven-prison sequence). This is an explicit fixed
practice order within the narrated job groups, not a live-DPS ranking or a claim
that WHM always contributes less than a shield healer. A party's job/burst plan
can legitimately produce another order; the chat alone does not justify silently
replacing the reference convention with one such plan.

Correction-pass Debug verification passed all 49 dedicated Mana P4 cases,
including all four native-proxy Motion movement checks and the Optical healer
placement check. These verify emitted native movement and fake-client readback,
not a rendered animation in the live client.
